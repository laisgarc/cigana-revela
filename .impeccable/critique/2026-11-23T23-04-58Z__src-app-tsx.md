## Design Specificity Verdict

The interface feels authored rather than templated. Its custom celestial-card artwork, wine/parchment palette, editorial serif typography, ethical “the choices remain yours” framing, and restrained copy create a coherent tarot-specific world. The proof layer is still interchangeable: without Raissa-specific evidence such as a portrait, voice, method, approved testimonials, or a representative reading, much of the page could belong to another tarot, astrology, or spiritual-wellness practitioner.

## Overall Impression

The site is elegant, calm, ethical, and unusually cohesive for its category. Its biggest missed opportunity is that atmosphere is doing the work that concrete trust and offer information should do. The visitor reaches a warm invitation to contact Raissa, but not a sufficiently informed reason to cross into WhatsApp.

## What’s Working

- The visual direction is distinctive: custom card art, delicate line work, editorial typography, and muted ruby/gold color create a memorable identity without stock mysticism.
- The ethical tone creates emotional safety. Copy such as “As escolhas continuam sendo suas” avoids certainty claims and pressure.
- Responsive and accessibility foundations are strong: no horizontal overflow at 390px, clear mobile CTA and menu, semantic sections, skip link, visible focus, reduced-motion handling, Escape-to-close, and inert handling for the hidden fixed CTA.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The offer withholds the information required to decide

The hero and reading card send price, duration, format, and options to WhatsApp, while the FAQ titled around cost and duration still does not answer them. This turns WhatsApp into a toll gate in a personal, trust-sensitive service. Publish reading formats, durations, delivery channel, starting or exact prices, payment methods, and basic cancellation/rescheduling terms. Use WhatsApp for availability and fit, not baseline product discovery.

Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Brand atmosphere substitutes for practitioner trust

Raissa is introduced through prose and a botanical illustration, but there is no portrait, testimonial, sample reading, specific method, or verifiable experience signal. Visitors are asked to discuss relationships and choices with someone who remains abstract. Add one authentic proof layer before or beside the offer: a real portrait or short video, an exact description of how she conducts readings, approved testimonials, or a representative anonymized example. Do not invent credentials.

Suggested command: `$impeccable bolder`

### [P2] The primary offer is buried by the information architecture

The header links to process, about, and FAQ, but not “Leituras.” The actual offer appears after themes, process, and about; the mobile page is 7,402px tall. Add “Leituras” to the navigation and move a compact, concrete offer block closer to the hero. Let process and biography support the offer afterward.

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`

### [P2] Repetition creates a conversion plateau

Seven CTA placements repeat nearly the same action while several sections recycle the same emotional promise. The page reassures repeatedly without adding proportional confidence. Restructure the sequence as promise → concrete offer → practitioner trust → process → FAQ → final CTA, with every section answering a new objection.

Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`

## Minor Observations

- The persistent mobile CTA is useful but cannot be minimized and duplicates the final CTA.
- External CTA copy should explicitly say that WhatsApp opens.
- All-caps eyebrow text becomes slightly dense at the smallest breakpoint.
- The custom card art is memorable; the botanical about illustration is comparatively generic.
- Apparent contrast is strong, but muted microcopy should receive a measured WCAG check.
- Cognitive load is moderate: each viewport is calm, but six FAQ siblings and a very long mobile journey create cumulative load.
- The emotional peak occurs in the hero; the page lacks an equally strong trust or information peak near conversion.

## Questions to Consider

- Is withholding public pricing a deliberate lead-qualification strategy, or an accidental trust tax?
- Before opening WhatsApp, should the visitor be choosing a reading, verifying personal fit, or merely starting a conversation?
- If only one Raissa-specific proof element could appear above the first CTA—portrait, testimonial, or sample reading—which would make the promise most credible?
- Could the page earn the same trust in half its current mobile length?
- What would make the final section feel like a payoff rather than a repetition of the hero?
